Reflections

What my Northbridge Financial co-op is teaching me about problem-solving, feedback, and the software I want to build.

SWOT strengths Emotional intelligence Professional development My vision

SWOT strengths

Problem-solving and adaptability are strengths I can point to in my co-op. Troubleshooting bugs gave me practice tracing unexpected behaviour. Moving from fixes to new features, then to developing an internal application from scratch, gave me a chance to apply that approach to broader requirements.

I want to build on those strengths by writing down what I know, what I’ve tried, and what I still need to clarify when I pick up an unfamiliar task.

  • Problem Analysis
  • Life-Long Learning

Diagnosing bugs draws on Problem Analysis. Applying what I learned to broader development work and identifying a habit to practice connect to Life-Long Learning.

Emotional intelligence

I demonstrated the application’s progress to a senior colleague and used their feedback to organize a development roadmap. That review showed me how a colleague’s perspective can help shape the work after a demo.

In future reviews, I want to pause before responding, ask which concern is most important, and summarize what I heard. That gives the other person a chance to correct my understanding before I commit to a change.

  • Communication Skills
  • Individual and Teamwork

Explaining my progress and checking my understanding of feedback use Communication Skills. Using a colleague’s input to plan my own development work connects individual ownership with teamwork.

Professional development

Taking ownership of an internal application gave me practice turning high-level requirements into functional modules. The roadmap gave me a way to carry feedback into the next stage of development.

I want to make that planning more consistent by tracking my weekly priorities and blockers and bringing concise updates to check-ins. I’ll use my co-op goals to review how often I follow through.

  • Professionalism
  • Life-Long Learning

Taking responsibility for follow-up after a review connects to Professionalism. Evaluating a new planning habit against measurable goals supports Life-Long Learning.

My Vision as an Engineer

I want to create software that simplifies people’s lives regardless of their technical experience. The YorkU Course Availability Notifier reflects that direction by automating repeated seat checks and sending an email when a seat opens.

Ethical engineering. I want to use plain language, support accessible interaction, and test with people who have different levels of technical experience. I also want to explain what data a feature needs and collect only what is necessary.

Sustainability. I want to limit unnecessary background processing and document how software works so it can be maintained and improved over time.

  • Ethics and Equity
  • Impact of Engineering on Society and Environment

Accessibility and responsible data collection reflect Ethics and Equity. Reducing unnecessary processing and considering who can use the software connect design decisions to their environmental and social impact.
